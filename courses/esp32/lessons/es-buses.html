Most sensors don't give you a voltage to read; they talk over a bus. Four cover nearly everything you'll plug in: I²C, 1-Wire, SPI and UART.

I²C: many devices on two wires

from machine import I2C, Pin
i2c = I2C(0, scl=Pin(22), sda=Pin(21), freq=100_000)
print([hex(a) for a in i2c.scan()])     # e.g. ['0x76'] for a BME280

Two wires (SDA data, SCL clock) plus power and ground, shared by every device; each has an address. A BME280 (temperature, humidity, pressure) is 0x76 or 0x77. Always scan first: an empty list means wiring, power or missing pull-ups, not your driver. Most breakout boards include the pull-up resistors I²C needs.

1-Wire: the DS18B20 temperature probe

import onewire, ds18x20, time
ds = ds18x20.DS18X20(onewire.OneWire(Pin(4)))
roms = ds.scan()                 # each probe has a unique 64-bit id
ds.convert_temp()
time.sleep_ms(750)               # a conversion takes up to 750 ms
for rom in roms:
    print(rom.hex(), ds.read_temp(rom))

One data wire with a 4.7 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V; several probes can share it, each found by its id. The waterproof DS18B20 on a cable is the fridge probe. A reading of 85.0 °C is its power-on value: the conversion didn't happen (too short a wait, or power trouble), not a hot fridge.

SPI and UART

  • SPI: faster, four wires (clock, data out, data in, chip select), one chip-select per device. Displays and SD cards.
  • UART: a serial line, TX to the other side's RX. Particle sensors (PMS5003), GPS modules, CO₂ sensors. machine.UART(1, baudrate=9600, tx=..., rx=...); the baud rate must match the device.

Drivers

Many sensors have MicroPython drivers: one .py file to copy to the board, some via mpremote mip install. Read the driver before trusting it: check units, check it raises on a failed read rather than returning 0, check it doesn't block for seconds.

Wiring that keeps working

I²C is meant for short distances on a board: a metre of cable is pushing it. For a sensor far from the board, 1-Wire copes with several metres, or put a small board next to the sensor. Breadboard contacts loosen; for anything that stays, solder or use screw terminals.

From the Pi

The same buses, with the same names: i2cdetect on the Pi is i2c.scan() here. Drivers for the Pi often use Linux's /dev/i2c-1; on the ESP32 you call the bus directly.

Trap

Believing a first reading. Sensors often need a moment after power-up, and the first value can be stale or a default (the DS18B20's 85 °C). Throw away the first, and range-check the rest.

Try it

Wire a DS18B20 with its 4.7 kΩ pull-up and print the temperature every 5 s. Then remove the resistor and see what the scan and readings do.
