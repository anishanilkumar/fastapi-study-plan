"An ESP32" can mean a chip, a module with that chip under a metal can, or a whole dev board with USB and pins. They're different things to buy, and the differences between family members decide what a project can do.

Chip, module, board

  • Chip: the silicon, e.g. ESP32-C3. Processor, Wi-Fi radio, RAM. Needs external flash and an antenna.
  • Module: chip + flash (often 4 MB) + crystal + antenna, under a shielded can. ESP32-WROOM-32, ESP32-C3-MINI-1. This is what's certified for radio use.
  • Dev board: a module on a board with a USB connector, a 3.3 V regulator, BOOT and RESET (EN) buttons, and pins on a 2.54 mm grid for a breadboard. ESP32-DevKitC, the many "ESP32-C3 SuperMini" clones. This is what you start with.

Almost all of them are Wi-Fi on 2.4 GHz only. If your router's 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks share one name, a board joins fine; if the house has only 5 GHz, it can't.

Memory, in numbers

Around 400 KB of RAM (some of it taken by Wi-Fi), 4 MB of flash typical. Boards with PSRAM (2 to 8 MB of extra, slower RAM) make MicroPython far roomier. Compared to the Pi: jarvis has a gigabyte and a Linux kernel; the ESP32 has a few hundred kilobytes and no operating system you'd recognise. A program is the whole device.

From the Pi

The Pi runs Linux: processes, files, SSH, systemd restarting things. The ESP32 runs one program. No SSH; you talk to it over USB serial, or over the network only if your program listens. If the program hangs, the device hangs, until a watchdog (lesson 11) or someone resets it.

Trap

Buying an ESP32-H2 or a bare chip for a Wi-Fi sensor, or a board with a charge-only USB cable in the box. Check the radio, and use a cable you know carries data.

Try it

Look up the board you'll buy: which chip, how much flash, PSRAM or not, native USB or a USB-serial chip (and which: CP2102, CH340), and where its BOOT button is.
