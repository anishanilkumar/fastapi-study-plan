Most "random" ESP32 problems are power: resets when Wi-Fi starts, a sensor that reads nonsense, a battery that's flat in a day. Ten minutes on power saves evenings of debugging code that's fine.

What it draws

StateRoughly
Wi-Fi transmittingPeaks of 200 to 400 mA, for milliseconds
Running, Wi-Fi connected80 to 150 mA on average
Light sleepAbout 1 mA
Deep sleep, the chip aloneAbout 10 µA
Deep sleep, a typical dev boardSeveral mA: its regulator, USB chip and power LED stay on

Those numbers vary by chip and board; measure yours with a USB power meter. The peaks matter most: they're what a weak supply can't deliver.

Brownouts

When the voltage dips too low (a thin USB cable, a weak charger, a long wire), the chip's brownout detector resets it, and the serial log says Brownout detector was triggered. Typically this happens exactly when Wi-Fi starts, so it looks like a Wi-Fi bug. Fixes: a better cable and supply, shorter wires, and a capacitor (100 µF or so) across 3.3 V and GND near the module.

Powering a board

  • USB (5 V) into the board's regulator: the easy way, for anything near a socket.
  • The 3V3 pin: a clean, regulated 3.3 V only. Bypasses the regulator.
  • The 5V / VIN pin: 5 V into the regulator. Never feed USB and VIN at once unless the board says it can.
  • A LiPo cell (3.7 V nominal, 4.2 V full): too high for the chip directly. Use a board with a battery connector and charger, or a low-quiescent regulator. Three AA alkaline cells start at 4.5 V; two are too low as they drain.

Sensors share the supply

Power sensors from the board's 3.3 V, with grounds connected: every device in the circuit needs a common ground, or signals between them mean nothing. A sensor powered from a separate supply with no shared ground is the classic "it reads garbage".

From the Pi

Same lesson as the Pi's undervoltage warnings and the NAS's flaky USB drive: under-powered hardware fails in ways that look like software. jarvis tells you in its kernel log; the ESP32 tells you once on the serial port, if you're watching.

Trap

Judging battery life from the chip's deep-sleep figure. A dev board's regulator and LED can draw a thousand times more than the sleeping chip. For a battery device, choose a board made for it, or measure.

Try it

With a USB power meter, note what your board draws idle, while connecting to Wi-Fi, and in machine.deepsleep(). Work out how long a 2000 mAh battery would last waking once every 10 minutes for 5 seconds.
