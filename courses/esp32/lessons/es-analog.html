Not everything is a bus. Some sensors give a voltage (a light sensor, a soil-moisture probe, a battery level), and some outputs need something between on and off (a dimmed LED, a fan's speed). That's the ADC and PWM.

Reading a voltage: the ADC

from machine import ADC, Pin
adc = ADC(Pin(34), atten=ADC.ATTN_11DB)    # full range, roughly 0 to 3.1 V
raw = adc.read_u16()                        # 0 to 65535
mv = adc.read_uv() // 1000                  # calibrated millivolts, where supported
  • Attenuation sets the range. Without it, only up to about 1 V reads correctly. 11 dB is the widest.
  • The ESP32's ADC is not very linear, especially near the ends of its range, and it's noisy. Use read_uv() (calibrated), average several reads, and don't expect better than a few percent.
  • On the original ESP32, use ADC1 pins (32 to 39); ADC2 is unusable while Wi-Fi is on.
  • Never put more than 3.3 V on a pin. To measure a battery, use a divider (two equal resistors halve it) and multiply back.

Something in between: PWM

from machine import PWM, Pin
led = PWM(Pin(2), freq=1000, duty_u16=16384)    # on a quarter of the time
led.duty_u16(65535)                              # fully on

PWM switches the pin on and off fast; the share of time it's on (the duty) looks like a level to an LED or a motor. 1 kHz suits LEDs; fans and motors want what their datasheet says (PC fans: 25 kHz). A servo wants 50 Hz with a pulse of 1 to 2 ms. The pin still gives only a few milliamps: a fan or a motor needs a MOSFET driven by the pin.

Touch

The original ESP32 and the S3 have capacitive touch pins: a bare wire or a coin becomes a button. machine.TouchPad(Pin(4)).read() drops when touched. Calibrate against the untouched value at start, since it drifts with humidity.

From the Pi

The Pi has no ADC at all; you'd add a chip on I²C. The ESP32 has one built in, good enough for "is it dark" or "is the battery low", not for measurement.

Trap

Using a cheap resistive soil-moisture probe powered all the time. It corrodes in weeks. Power it from a pin only while reading, or buy a capacitive one.

Try it

Read a potentiometer (or a light sensor) with the ADC and set an LED's PWM duty from it. Average 16 reads and see how much steadier it is.
